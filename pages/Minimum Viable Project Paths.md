# Step 8 Deep Research: Minimum Viable Project Paths for the Remaining Urban-ABM Candidates
- ## Executive summary
  
  This review changes the decision rule from **“keep a model unless we can prove it should be excluded”** to **“keep a model only when a credible semester project path can be positively demonstrated.”** Each of the 17 remaining candidates was therefore tested on four items:
  
  **A — Model kernel:** a bounded part of the published model that could realistically become the semester project.  
  **B — Data path:** specific empirical data that are genuinely obtainable rather than merely mentioned in the paper.  
  **C — Validation path:** observations not used for calibration that can test the model quantitatively.  
  **D — Implementation path:** usable public code, or a sufficiently small reimplementation.
  
  The evidence standard is deliberately stricter than in Steps 6–7. In particular, a paper saying that it was “validated” is not enough if the underlying validation data cannot realistically be obtained for our project.
  
  The result is a much more useful reduction:
  
  | Step-8 status | Models | Interpretation |
  |---|---:|---|
  | **Shortlist** | **5** | All four project-path components are convincingly available |
  | **Hold** | **7** | Attractive, but one bounded gap still needs resolution |
  | **Exclude** | **5** | At least one major project-path gap |
  | **Total** | **17** | |
  
  The five cleanest Step-8 survivors are **Geneva DPSIR/TRACES, HUEM Wallonia, Tallinn CA–Agent, MATSim Open Berlin, and eqasim Île-de-France**. After family-level dominance pruning, I would retain three additional **conditional finalists** because they offer distinct and potentially valuable project types: **RESMOBcity Leipzig, ENERGY Pro Amsterdam, and Hamburg Pedestrian Flow**.
  
  That gives a final research shortlist of **eight**, but importantly it is not an undifferentiated eight:
  
  > **Fully evidenced:** Geneva DPSIR/TRACES · HUEM Wallonia · Tallinn CA–Agent · MATSim Open Berlin · eqasim Île-de-France  
  > **Conditional finalists:** RESMOBcity Leipzig · ENERGY Pro Amsterdam · Hamburg Pedestrian Flow
  
  This is a substantially stronger basis for the next stage than the previous 17-model set. It also reveals an important pattern: **code availability is no longer the main bottleneck**. The recurring bottleneck is now **independent empirical validation data at the same spatial/behavioral resolution as the ABM**.

  A key methodological point is that **“reimplementable” can satisfy D**. Lack of code is therefore not itself fatal. HUEM and Tallinn are examples: their published mechanisms are sufficiently bounded that a clean Python spatial implementation is more realistic than reproducing a large legacy framework. Conversely, an open framework does not automatically pass: the framework must lead to a concrete empirical project rather than a semester spent learning infrastructure.
- ## Model-by-model Minimum Viable Project Paths
  
  **RESMOBcity Leipzig — Hold.**  
  **A:** The appropriate kernel is not the full shrinking-city model but the loop **household relocation → neighborhood choice → dwelling occupancy/vacancy**. Household types, residential attractiveness, and housing availability should remain; demolition, complex urban-development scenarios, and nonessential demographic modules can be omitted. Complexity is **medium**. UFZ describes RESMOBcity as a spatial ABM based on empirical household types and reports that it can reproduce observed population patterns, intra-urban migration, and residential vacancy in Leipzig. UFZ also still exposes the software as a downloadable Java application, originally requiring Java 1.6+. citeturn2view3 **B:** population/household structure and housing stock can be drawn from [Leipzig municipal statistics](https://statistik.leipzig.de/) and German Census data; the remaining uncertainty is whether a sufficiently fine, longitudinal **intra-urban movement + vacancy** series is openly downloadable rather than only published in aggregates. **C:** calibrate on an earlier interval and validate neighborhood/Ortsteil population change, move flows and vacancy in later years using MAE/RMSE and spatial residual statistics. **D:** reuse/port the UFZ Java model rather than rebuild all infrastructure; **~3–5 person-weeks**, or ~5–6 for a clean Python/Mesa port. The unresolved B detail is why this remains **Hold**, not a full pass. citeturn2view3turn0search3
  
  **Vienna household model — Hold.**  
  **A:** Reduce the Gaube–Remesch model to **heterogeneous households selecting residential locations under a small number of urban-planning scenarios**, at census-district rather than dwelling resolution. That preserves the substantive ABM mechanism while removing unnecessary citywide detail. The original paper explicitly models the effect of urban planning on household residential decisions in Vienna. citeturn14search3 **B:** Vienna is exceptionally attractive empirically: the city publishes building, dwelling and resident counts for its **250 census districts**, including register-census information for 2011 and more recent years; Statistik Austria likewise maintains annually updated building and dwelling register statistics. citeturn21search1turn21search9turn21search25turn21search29 Useful entry point: [Vienna building and housing statistics](https://www.wien.gv.at/statistik/gebaeude-wohnungen). **C:** 2011 can seed/calibrate a spatial distribution and 2021+ can test later household/dwelling distributions, but this mainly validates **outcomes**, not the behavioral relocation rule itself; that is the remaining weakness. **D:** no usable public study code was found; a focused Python/Mesa + GeoPandas implementation is nevertheless realistic at **~4–6 person-weeks**. Because C remains materially weaker than for the leading residential models, this is **Hold**.
  
  **ReMoTe-S — Hold.**  
  **A:** This is one of the cleanest kernels in the set: preserve **household life-course change → housing satisfaction → decision to move → allocation to an available dwelling**, and reduce the multidimensional “desired housing functions” to the few dimensions actually needed for the RQ. The published model was constructed from empirical work with three multifamily housing providers and explicitly links life-course and satisfaction to mobility. citeturn2view1 **B:** the executable/model archive and analysis notebook are publicly archived at CoMSES, but the deeper behavioral parameterization rests partly on provider/survey evidence; I did not find equally strong evidence that all of those underlying microdata are openly reusable. citeturn2view1turn2view2 Code: [CoMSES ReMoTe-S release](https://www.comses.net/codebases/a6160a80-0c4d-492a-bbb3-cd08964b18ff/releases/1.0.0/). **C:** the paper reports plausibility checks, empirical correspondence and sensitivity analysis, but not a clean temporal out-of-sample validation of the sort we now want. citeturn2view1 **D:** Python 3.9 implementation is already available, so adaptation is only **~2–3 person-weeks**. This is an excellent technical candidate, but its empirical preference-data/validation issue is exactly the kind of gap Step 8 is intended to expose.
  
  **COMPASS — Exclude.**  
  The internal corpus previously treated COMPASS as a compact behavioral model with an open implementation and an inference pipeline. In this deep-verification pass, however, targeted searches did **not** allow me to unambiguously reconnect the shorthand “COMPASS” to a specific primary Urban-ABM publication and corresponding official repository without risking conflation with unrelated COMPASS projects. Under the new positive-evidence rule, that is decisive. **A:** the precise published kernel cannot be documented safely. **B/C:** the previously noted register-data and out-of-sample issues cannot be audited against a primary source. **D:** exact repository link **not found**. This is not a judgment that the model is scientifically poor; it is a **provenance failure for our current selection process**. **Exclude until the original bibliographic identity is restored.**
  
  **Stockholm residential segregation — Exclude.**  
  The same stricter logic applies here. The previous corpus identified a Stockholm residential-segregation ABM and already flagged register-data access as the practical bottleneck. In this research pass, the candidate label was not sufficient to recover a unique primary publication/code/data package with enough confidence to document A–D. **A:** a reduced household relocation/segregation kernel is conceptually plausible, but that alone is not positive evidence. **B:** the central empirical advantage of Swedish administrative microdata becomes a disadvantage for a semester replication if the required person/household-level register access cannot be reproduced by the student team. **C:** no independently reproducible validation series was established. **D:** repo **not found**. Since both provenance and empirical access are major gaps rather than one small uncertainty, **Exclude**.
  
  **Geneva DPSIR / TRACES — Shortlist.**  
  **A:** Keep only **commuter-household relocation in response to public-transport travel time, housing cost and housing availability**. Do not implement the broader TRACES ambition of endogenous transport-network generation or land-cover feedback. This creates a medium-sized spatial ABM with a very clear urban mechanism. The 2024 paper explicitly models residential choices and mobility for the canton of Geneva using real geographical and public-transport information; a related TRACES preprint provides supplementary material through a UNIGE GitLab repository. citeturn16search25turn19search1turn17search32 **B:** Geneva's SITG is unusually strong: the canton provides downloadable **quarterly resident population at statistical sub-sector level** and openly distributed mobility-network layers. citeturn20search5turn20search6 Data: [SITG population by statistical sub-sector](https://sitg.ge.ch/donnees/ocs-population-ssecteur). **C:** calibrate on one baseline year/quarter and validate subsequent spatial population redistribution and accessibility/commuting patterns at sub-sector level; suitable metrics include population-change MAE, rank/spatial correlation and residual clustering. **D:** the supplementary model materials reduce risk; a bounded Python reimplementation is also plausible at **~3–5 person-weeks**. The article's data-availability statement says the study data are available, further strengthening reproducibility. citeturn17search0 **Shortlist.**
  
  **Vienna-region spatial-agent growth — Hold.**  
  **A:** From the legacy candidate in our corpus, the sensible reduction is a single **suburban urban-growth/densification mechanism**: raster parcels/cells change state according to suitability plus one spatial decision-making actor layer. Restricting the study to one suburban corridor prevents the original regional architecture from becoming the project itself. **B:** current Austrian population, buildings and dwellings are strongly supported by register data; Statistik Austria describes the building/dwelling census as a full administrative-register enumeration and publishes recent building and dwelling statistics. citeturn21search5turn21search13turn21search29 These can be combined with open historical land-cover imagery for the spatial state. **C:** a land-cover time split makes independent validation straightforward: calibrate on an early transition and assess later built-up-cell transitions with precision/recall, IoU/Figure of Merit and spatial-pattern statistics. **D:** the problem is provenance: no modern runnable repository for the specific legacy model was recovered. A fresh Python/GeoPandas/raster implementation is still plausible at **~5–7 person-weeks**, but sufficiently much interpretation of the old model would be required that this remains **Hold**. It is also dominated by HUEM/Tallinn below.
  
  **HUEM Wallonia — Shortlist.**  
  **A:** HUEM offers an unusually defensible ABM kernel because the published architecture explicitly couples **logistic suitability, cellular-automata neighborhood dynamics and actor decisions**. A semester version should retain the three key decision roles—developer, landowner/farmer, and planning authority—while restricting the outcome to **whether/where a cell converts to urban use**. The paper was designed specifically as a hybrid urban-expansion model rather than as a generic city simulator. citeturn3search0 Complexity is **medium**. **B:** the required variables are unusually conventional: historical built-up/land-use state, roads/accessibility, existing urban adjacency, population pressure and planning constraints. These can be reconstructed from Walloon/Belgian open geodata plus Copernicus land-cover products rather than confidential individual-level records. **C:** this model naturally supports the validation design we want: estimate/calibrate on an earlier land-use transition and predict a genuinely later map; assess transition precision/recall, IoU/Figure of Merit, quantity disagreement and spatial allocation error. **D:** a public official implementation was **not found**, but the reduced architecture is implementable in Python using NumPy/rasterio/GeoPandas and a light agent layer in roughly **5–7 person-weeks**. Crucially, HUEM also remains one of the strongest models from our earlier K7 screen because its hybrid agent component is meaningful relative to simpler logit/CA alternatives. **Shortlist.** citeturn3search0
  
  **Tallinn CA–Agent — Shortlist.**  
  **A:** The paper couples cellular automata, agent-based logic and a Markov component for urban expansion. A semester kernel can keep **cell agents + local transition rules + suitability/accessibility + Markov-derived overall transition pressure**, omitting the full scenario suite. citeturn22search1 **B:** this is one of the best remote-sensing data paths in the set: the study reconstructs Tallinn-region land-use/built-up change over **1990–2018**, with historical LULC states available at multiple points in that period; the same type of inputs can be regenerated from open satellite imagery and open spatial covariates. citeturn22search1 **C:** use the earlier transition for calibration and reserve the later period as a true temporal holdout—for example, calibration on 1990→2006 and validation on 2006→2018—using transition precision/recall, IoU/Figure of Merit and morphology/fragmentation statistics. This is substantially cleaner than calibration and evaluation against the same endpoint. **D:** no official reusable model repo was identified, but this architecture is relatively compact: **~4–6 person-weeks** in Python/rasterio/NumPy, optionally Mesa. **Shortlist.** The remaining scientific question is not feasibility but whether the agent layer yields enough additional insight over a well-designed CA; that should remain visible when formulating the eventual RQ.
  
  **Valladolid domestic-water ABM — Exclude.**  
  **A:** A defensible reduction exists: keep **household water consumption by dwelling type + conservation/opinion diffusion + adoption of water-saving technology**, and drop the residential urban-dynamics submodel. **B:** unfortunately, the original model's most valuable empirical input is also its main reproducibility problem. The paper says the initialization used a regional **water-supplier consumption database for 1997–2006**, a georeferenced socioeconomic database from Valladolid City Council, the 2003 urban plan, regional orthophotography and the 2001 INE census. citeturn22search2 The census, planning and mapping pieces can be replaced with open modern equivalents, but an equivalent household/block-level longitudinal water-consumption dataset has not been demonstrated as publicly accessible. **C:** the original validation design was actually respectable: the model was parameterized with first-quarter 2006 data and compared with the following two quarters. citeturn22search2 But that validation cannot simply be reproduced without the supplier data. **D:** the original was Java/Repast; a reduced Python/Mesa or Repast implementation would take **~5–7 person-weeks**. Because B is not peripheral but central to calibration and C, **Exclude**.
  
  **ENERGY Pro Amsterdam — Hold.**  
  **A:** Keep **homeowner agents deciding whether to adopt one or two energy-efficiency retrofit measures**, with household heterogeneity, building context and neighborhood/social influence. Remove the broader Positive Energy District accounting and the full menu of technologies. This is a very good semester-scale behavioral kernel. The published ENERGY Pro model is empirically and spatially explicit and studies Amsterdam homeowners' retrofit decisions. citeturn24search8turn24search10 **B:** this is one of the strongest implementation/data packages: the public repository contains the NetLogo model, R spatial-microsimulation code and data-processing directories; the research also references a deposited synthetic population of Amsterdam households. citeturn24search2turn24search8 Repo: [github.com/erkinaiderkenbaeva/Energy-Pro-model](https://github.com/erkinaiderkenbaeva/Energy-Pro-model). The repository is Apache-2.0 and currently consists primarily of NetLogo plus R. citeturn24search2 **C:** the unresolved point is a genuinely independent **temporal** retrofit-adoption benchmark at buurt/neighborhood level. Energy-label/building data offer promising routes, but I would verify the longitudinal coverage before treating them as a clean holdout. **D:** because code and population-generation machinery already exist, **~2–4 person-weeks** is realistic for a reduced experiment. **Hold only because of C**; otherwise this is one of the strongest candidates.
  
  **Hamburg pedestrian-flow ABM — Hold.**  
  **A:** Reduce to one inner-city district and retain **amenity/land-use-based trip generation + heterogeneous pedestrians + movement through the street network**. Avoid the paper's full planned-versus-existing urban-design application. Complexity is **low–medium**. **B:** land-use/amenity and network inputs can be rebuilt from accessible spatial data; the original paper itself uses land-use floor-space information to model pedestrian activity in inner-city Hamburg. citeturn23search0turn23search12 **C:** scientifically, this candidate is particularly attractive because the authors actually compare flows simulated for the existing district with **real-world pedestrian activity measurements for external validation**. citeturn23search0turn23search12 A semester design could calibrate on some counter locations/times and validate on held-out counters using rank correlation, normalized RMSE and flow-share errors. The only reason for △ is operational: I did not establish that the exact raw counter series from the paper is openly downloadable today. **D:** study code was **not found**, but this narrow model can be rebuilt with Python/Mesa + NetworkX/OSMnx in about **3–4 person-weeks**. **Hold**, with one very concrete check: obtain a public pedestrian-count benchmark before committing.
  
  **5aDay Paris — Exclude.**  
  **A:** A reduced kernel could in principle model **daily urban mobility/social exposure and dietary behavior** for only part of the Paris region rather than the original very large synthetic population. However, downscaling is not neutral here: social exposure and mixing are themselves part of the phenomenon, so aggressive truncation risks changing the mechanism being studied. The primary publication is recent and uses a Paris-region agent population for an urban/behavioral-health question. citeturn6search0 **B/C:** the empirical path relies on behavioral/health information in addition to spatial population and mobility data; this pass did not establish a clean open set of longitudinal microdata sufficient both for calibration and an independent behavioral holdout. **D:** our previous corpus indicated freely available model/analysis material, but I was not able in this pass to recover a sufficiently unambiguous current official code link to cite. Marking it **“not found”** is preferable to inventing one. With two substantive uncertainties plus the scale sensitivity, **Exclude** for this project, even though the research question itself is interesting.
  
  **MATSim Open Berlin — Shortlist.**  
  **A:** Do **not** build a Berlin transport model. Use the existing **1% scenario** and modify one bounded behavioral/policy component—for example one mode-choice response, a pricing/intervention scenario, or accessibility response—while treating the rest of MATSim as infrastructure. That makes this a medium rather than massive project. **B:** the official repository provides the scenario, its input infrastructure and multiple sample sizes; the documentation lists **10%, 3%, and 1%** versions. citeturn10view0 Repo: [github.com/matsim-scenarios/matsim-berlin](https://github.com/matsim-scenarios/matsim-berlin). **C:** the published scenario is already checked against traffic counts, modal split and trip-distance distributions, giving us several empirical targets. citeturn10view0 For our K8 standard, we should reserve count stations or a second observation period rather than simply reuse every calibration target. **D:** Java/Maven instructions are documented, large inputs are hosted externally, and the project has continued to publish releases; a release was available in 2026. citeturn10view0turn10view1 Setup plus one focused experimental module is **~3–5 person-weeks**. Main risk is not feasibility but letting MATSim configuration dominate the scientific question. **Shortlist.**
  
  **eqasim Île-de-France — Shortlist.**  
  **A:** The viable kernel is **not** the entire Paris transport system. Use a prepared or generated synthetic population, restrict it to a manageable geographic/sample subset, and study one behavior/policy mechanism through the MATSim interface. Complexity remains **medium–high**, but bounded. **B:** eqasim is unusually strong here: its official `eqasim-france` repository explicitly creates an open synthetic population of Île-de-France from multiple publicly available sources, including persons, households, activities, locations, trip times and modes; the documentation states that a user with basic Python knowledge can regenerate it from scratch and can create MATSim input. citeturn23search5 Repo: [github.com/eqasim-org/eqasim-france](https://github.com/eqasim-org/eqasim-france). **C:** demographic marginals, activity/trip distributions and mobility indicators can be checked against input-independent published aggregates, with traffic counts as a further spatial benchmark. The 2021 reference was explicitly constructed around an open/publicly available synthetic travel-demand pipeline. citeturn23search5turn9search20 **D:** Python data preparation plus Java/MATSim means a higher learning burden than ENERGY Pro or Geneva; estimate **~4–6 person-weeks**. **Shortlist**, especially if synthetic-population construction itself is a desired learning outcome.
  
  **mobiTopp — Hold.**  
  **A:** Keep only a **small weekly activity/travel-demand kernel**, ideally using an existing scenario and focusing on one household interaction or mode-choice question rather than rebuilding population synthesis, destination choice and network assignment simultaneously. mobiTopp is an open-source Java agent-based travel-demand framework and supports week-long simulation. citeturn8search10turn8search34 **B:** an open Rastatt application/scenario exists, which is much more realistic for a semester than starting from a blank German city. citeturn8search2 **C:** this is the weak point: the cleanest independent benchmark for a particular reduced experiment still depends on which component is selected, and some detailed German travel-survey data have access conditions. I therefore cannot yet write a single strong calibration→holdout design comparable with Tallinn or Open Berlin. **D:** code is open and modular, but the Java framework and weekly activity structure still imply **~4–6 person-weeks** before a substantive extension is complete. **Hold**; in the transport family it is presently dominated by Open Berlin and, for open-data reproducibility, eqasim.
  
  **Zurich MATSim transport-planning — Exclude.**  
  **A:** A generic MATSim Zurich kernel is certainly possible, but that is not enough: Step 8 requires a bounded path tied to the **published candidate model**. **B/C/D:** MATSim itself is open, but this research pass did not recover a complete, clearly identified public package containing the exact Zurich study scenario, corresponding historical input data and validation targets. Exact study repo: **not found**. Reconstructing a contemporary Zurich MATSim scenario from Swiss population, network and timetable data would be a legitimate new research project, but it would no longer be a small extension/reproduction of this candidate and would likely require **6–8+ person-weeks** just to reach a trustworthy baseline. Because Open Berlin and eqasim supply comparable transport learning opportunities with far lower provenance risk, **Exclude**.
- ## Redundancy and dominance pruning
  
  Step 8 is deliberately followed by **family-level dominance**, because keeping five versions of essentially the same methodological project would defeat the purpose of the shortlist. Dominance here is not a crude point score. A candidate is removed when another model offers a broadly comparable scientific learning opportunity while providing a materially better combination of **data, validation and implementation path**.
  
  | Research family | Candidates | Winner(s) | Why |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | **Residential choice / relocation** | RESMOBcity, Vienna household, ReMoTe-S, COMPASS, Stockholm, Geneva | **Geneva + RESMOBcity** | Geneva has the best modern open spatial-data path; RESMOBcity supplies a distinct housing/vacancy-feedback model and public legacy software. ReMoTe-S is technically excellent but its preference-data path is weaker. |
  | **Urban expansion / land-use change** | Vienna-region, HUEM, Tallinn | **HUEM + Tallinn** | HUEM gives the clearest actor-based planning/land-conversion mechanism; Tallinn gives the cleanest remotely sensed temporal holdout and simpler spatial implementation. Vienna-region adds less while carrying greater legacy risk. |
  | **Household sustainability behavior** | Valladolid, ENERGY Pro, 5aDay | **ENERGY Pro** | Open code + spatial microsimulation + bounded homeowner decision problem dominate Valladolid's inaccessible water-consumption data and 5aDay's scale/behavioral-data burden. |
  | **Pedestrian urban dynamics** | Hamburg | **Hamburg, conditional** | Unique methodological niche; narrow model and genuine external observations, subject to securing accessible pedestrian-count data. |
  | **Agent-based transport** | Open Berlin, eqasim, mobiTopp, Zurich MATSim | **Open Berlin + eqasim** | Berlin is the quickest path to a calibrated runnable transport ABM; eqasim provides the strongest transparent synthetic-population/data pipeline. mobiTopp has less clear validation for a reduced project; Zurich lacks the exact study package. |
  
  The most important comparison is the **urban-growth family**. HUEM and Tallinn may superficially look redundant because both simulate urban expansion, but they offer different learning goals. HUEM is the better option if the project should focus on **heterogeneous urban actors and planning decisions**; Tallinn is the cleaner option if the project should focus on **spatial dynamics, remote sensing, calibration and temporal validation**. The two therefore deserve to survive together. citeturn3search0turn22search1
  
  Likewise, Open Berlin and eqasim are both transport candidates but not identical project experiences. Open Berlin minimizes baseline-building by starting from an already calibrated urban MATSim scenario with small samples; eqasim puts more emphasis on creating and understanding an open synthetic population and travel-demand pipeline. citeturn10view0turn23search5
- ## Recommended final shortlist
  
  I would carry **eight** models into the next, full-paper/code-review stage. Five already satisfy Step 8 cleanly; three are retained conditionally because their remaining gap is narrow enough to resolve quickly and because they add valuable diversity.

- ## Implications for the project decision
  
  This Step-8 exercise also answers the problem that motivated it: **why did our previous gates eliminate so few models?**
  
  The earlier logic was asymmetric:
  
  > *A candidate survived unless we could prove something was wrong.*
  
  Step 8 reverses that burden:
  
  > *A candidate survives only when we can describe a credible kernel, name the data, name the independent validation observations, and explain how the implementation gets built.*
  
  That change exposes several different kinds of failure that our previous screening could not separate:
  
  **Provenance failure:** COMPASS and Stockholm cannot currently be traced confidently enough from our shorthand candidate names to an auditable primary implementation/data package.
  
  **Empirical-access failure:** Valladolid is scientifically detailed and even has a sensible historical validation design, but its critical water-supplier database is not an evidenced open input for us. The paper calibrated on first-quarter 2006 consumption and checked the following two quarters, but that does not help our semester project if the underlying consumption series cannot be obtained. citeturn22search2
  
  **Reproduction-package failure:** Zurich MATSim is plausible in principle, but “MATSim is open” is not equivalent to “the exact published Zurich experiment is reproducible.”
  
  **Validation-data failure:** ENERGY Pro and Hamburg are much closer to surviving because the missing item is narrow and testable: establish one independent empirical endpoint before choosing them.
  
  **Dominance failure:** Vienna-region and mobiTopp may be individually feasible, but once HUEM/Tallinn and Open Berlin/eqasim are available, respectively, there is little reason to spend scarce deep-reading capacity on the weaker alternatives.
  
  That is the key methodological gain from Step 8. We are no longer manufacturing more abstract criteria to force the list downward. We are asking whether each candidate already contains the ingredients of an **actual bachelor-semester research project**.
